# Legal pages: the plan for designed DPA and Privacy Policy pages

Status: **planned, waiting on three decisions** (below). Nothing is built. Recorded 13 Oct 2026 on
`feat/trust-center-verbatim`.

The Trust Center links two ThingsBoard Cloud legal documents: the DPA
(`/products/paas/dpa/`) and the Privacy Policy (`/products/paas/privacy-policy/`). Both are bare
pages today. The goal is designed versions in the site's shell **without changing a single word of
the legal text**.

## What is there today

- **Word for word, the repo is what is live.** Checked 13 Oct 2026 by comparing the visible text of
  thingsboard.io with the repo's build: DPA 3,208 words, Privacy Policy 1,066 words, no differences.
- **Both use `src/layouts/LegalLayout.astro`**: a bare HTML document with no site header, footer or
  navigation, an 860px centred column, justified text. The same layout serves Terms of Use, the EU
  versions, the IoT Hub and demo policies, the EULA and the `/legal/` MDX documents (BUSL, the
  License Portal privacy policy).
- **One recorded decision:** #549 (July 2026) removed the cookie banner and the UTM tracker from
  `LegalLayout`. The commit gives no reason.
- **Hand-made markup:** spacing by `<br />`, clause numbers typed into the text ("1.1.8.1"), nested
  clauses as flat `<li>`s, headings as `<p><b>`.
- **Duplicated text:** the US and EU Cloud privacy policies (`products/paas/privacy-policy.astro`,
  `products/paas/eu/privacy-policy.astro`) are word for word the same (1,066 words) but two
  hand-written files.
- **Gaps to raise with legal:**
  - The DPA's own title is "Data Processing **Agreement**"; the Trust Center calls it "Data
    Processing Addendum (DPA)". The Trust Center should use the document's name.
  - The DPA has no effective date or version anywhere.

## Why they are plain (likely, not documented)

1. **Tracking.** The Cloud Privacy Policy, clause 2.5: we do not use third-party services ("such
   as Google Analytics") to analyse this data, and the policy covers "ThingsBoard Cloud Platform and
   related web pages". The site's `BaseLayout` loads Google Tag Manager (and through it Facebook and
   LinkedIn tags after consent). Serving the policy in that shell invites the question whether the
   page contradicts itself. Most likely what #549 was about.
2. **A stable document.** The DPA "forms part of" customers' agreements, so its URL is cited in
   contracts. A self-contained page reads, prints and archives the same through any redesign.
3. **Product flows** may open these pages (Cloud sign-up, the app). Not verifiable from this repo.
4. **History:** pasted from Word.

## Best practices this plan follows

- **One URL and one source per document.** A designed version renders the same source, never a
  retyped copy.
- **Legal owns the text; a check enforces it.** A comparison of the rendered text with the approved
  text fails on any difference.
- **Versions:** a visible effective date and version; previous versions kept at their own addresses;
  a short change log (the DPA promises notice of material changes).
- **Readable and parseable without CSS or JavaScript:** real headings; typed clause numbers kept as
  text (never generated by CSS: they are part of the legal text and must survive copying); an
  anchor per clause; nothing injected by scripts.
- **Readability:** left-aligned text, full-contrast body (the lists are grey today).
- **No marketing tracking, chat widgets or experiments** on legal pages.
- **Print, and a PDF of the DPA** for countersigning.

## Issues with the site's shell

Not a problem in itself; the issues are:

1. **Tracking:** needs a shell variant that loads no tracking at all (no GTM, not even after
   consent).
2. **Noise:** hide the promo strip and the help widget.
3. **Print:** the shell must drop out in print (the site does since #696).
4. **Product flows:** keep a plain version for apps to link to, if they do.

## The plan

1. **One source per document**, the existing markup moved out unchanged (for example
   `src/content/legal/paas-dpa.html`), rendered by both:
   - the **existing plain page**, its output word for word the same (checked);
   - a **designed page** in the shell variant without tracking: the document's own title, an
     effective date and version line (the DPA's a CLARIFY until legal gives one), "On this page"
     built from the existing clause headings without changing their text, clause anchors, a Print
     button, readable type, print styles.
2. **`pnpm lint:legal`**: the visible text of the designed page, the plain page and an approved
   snapshot (committed) must match word for word; any difference fails.
3. The Trust Center calls the DPA by its real name.

## Decisions needed

1. **Tracking:** the designed pages on a shell variant that loads no tracking at all?
   (Recommended.)
2. **Where:** prototype under `/trust-center/` (noindex) until legal approves, then replace the
   plain pages at their existing URLs?
3. **The source move:** move the text out of the existing legal page files so both versions read
   one source (output proven identical), or leave those files untouched and extract the text from
   them at build time (avoids touching them, but fragile)?
